Acoustic Levitation - Dissertation

Project is in the process of being published.

Ultrasonic Phased-Array Levitator: An embedded system capable of levitating and manipulating matter in mid-air using real-time acoustic beamforming, powered by an STM32 microcontroller and shift registers on a custom-made PCB. 

For my undergraduate dissertation, I focused on addressing a bottleneck in acoustic levitation: accessibility. Most advanced phased arrays rely on FPGA-based hardware, which offers high precision but introduces significant cost and development complexity. Meanwhile, existing microcontroller alternatives often struggle with limited I/O channels and coarse phase resolution. My goal was to design and build an accessible, low-cost system that could achieve deterministic, high-resolution 3D acoustic trapping using a general-purpose microcontroller.

Acoustic levitation relies on ultrasonic standing waves to generate stable pressure nodes. At these nodes, the acoustic radiation force overcomes gravity, enabling contactless suspension of small, lightweight objects. Unlike fixed-geometry levitators, an active phased array dynamically shifts the phase of each transducer. This enables real-time acoustic beamforming, allowing the focal point—and the trapped object—to be steered dynamically in three dimensions.

System Architecture & Hardware Implementation

  • Real-Time Phase Calculation: The STM32G474 calculates the required phase shifts for a targeted 3D coordinate and quantizes each 40 kHz cycle into 16 discrete time steps. 
  •  DMA & High-Speed SPI: Rather than toggling pins via CPU instructions, a Direct Memory Access (DMA) channel streams pre-computed phase tables continuously from memory to the SPI peripheral at 10.25 Mbit/s. This keeps CPU overhead minimal during operation. 
  •  Hardware Deserialization: Cascaded 74VHC595 shift registers capture the serial data and convert it to parallel outputs, latched precisely once per phase step by a hardware timer tied to the SPI clock. 
  •  Transducer Driver Stage: Because logic gates cannot deliver the current required by ultrasonic transducers, dedicated MIC4127 dual low-side MOSFET drivers amplify the signals up to the required drive voltage. 

Acoustic Field Validation

To verify the synthesized pressure fields, I built an automated measurement rig using a modified 3D-printer gantry driven by custom Python and G-code scripts on a 1 mm grid. To avoid spatial averaging from wide sensors, I used a Murata MA40S4S receiver with a 3D-printed PETG waveguide nozzle featuring a 1.5 mm aperture. The measured signals closely matched numerical Huygens-Fresnel simulations across both horizontal and vertical planes, successfully confirming accurate 3D focal steering—such as shifting from the centre (0,0,20) mm to an off-axis (5,5,20) mm target.

Levitation Results

For physical levitation, I built an opposed setup with two synchronized 3x3 arrays separated by 40 mm. The system stably trapped a 2 mm polystyrene sphere and steered it along pre-programmed 3D paths. While the compact array size limits the workspace and dynamic translation speed, it validates that microcontroller SPI/DMA serialization provides an effective, low-cost platform for active acoustic manipulation, with head-room to scale up to larger arrays and finer phase steps (theoretically up to pi/57 at 73 Mbit/s).
